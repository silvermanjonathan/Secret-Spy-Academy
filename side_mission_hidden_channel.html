The Hidden Channel

Every cipher so far screams "I am a secret." This mission whispers. Steganography hides the message inside an innocent-looking one, and the enemy who reads the cover text sees weather reports and grocery lists. No cipher, no key, just a channel: every fifth character, counted by a pattern you already own.

Drill 1 · The extractorE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Every fifth character, starting at zero

An intercepted note reads like a scrap of a map description. Agents who know the channel run a counter over it and keep only the characters where i % 5 is 0. Spaces count; every character ticks the counter.

cover = "NEAR OAKS OPEN NORTH"
secret = ""
i = 0
for letter in cover:
    if i % 5 == 0:
        secret = secret + letter
    i = i + 1
print(secret)
Predict The cover has 20 characters. Which positions fire the gate, and what do those four characters spell?
Positions 0, 5, 10, and 15: the N of NEAR, the O of OAKS, the O of OPEN, and the N of NORTH. NOON. The enemy who reads the note sees directions to nowhere; the agent who knows the channel reads a time. Nothing was encrypted, which is the whole point and the whole risk: steganography's secret is not a key but a location, and a secret location, once guessed, protects nothing.

Drill 2 · The wrong channelE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Right note, wrong count

A rival agency suspects the note and tries a channel of 7. Same extractor, one number changed.

cover = "NEAR OAKS OPEN NORTH"
secret = ""
i = 0
for letter in cover:
    if i % 7 == 0:
        secret = secret + letter
    i = i + 1
print(secret)
Predict Which positions fire now, how many characters come out of a 20-character cover, and do they spell anything?
Positions 0, 7, and 14: three characters, N, K, and a space, spelling nothing. The rivals shrug and move on, and that shrug is the defense working: a wrong channel yields plausible garbage, not a locked door begging to be forced. Compare the failure modes: a cipher intercepted announces there is a secret; a channel misread announces there is nothing. Quiet is its own kind of armor.

Drill 3 · Casing the channelE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

How many slots does a cover text offer?

Before writing a cover, an agent counts its capacity: on channel 5, a text of length n carries a secret letter at every position where the gate fires. The counter tells you before you write a word.

cover_length = 34
capacity = 0
i = 0
while i < 34:
    if i % 5 == 0:
        capacity = capacity + 1
    i = i + 1
print("Slots for secret letters:", capacity)
Predict Positions 0 through 33, gate at every multiple of 5: how many firings? And to smuggle a 6-letter secret on channel 5, at least how long must the cover be?
Seven: 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30. And a 6-letter secret needs its sixth slot at position 25, so the cover must run at least 26 characters: five secret letters fully spaced, plus one more character to stand on. Capacity math first, prose second; agents who write the cover before counting the slots end up cramming, and crammed covers read strangely, which is the one thing a cover must never do.

Field Mission · The Innocent Note

Write a cover that carries your secret

Part A, the worksheet: pick a 4-letter secret. On paper, write its letters in slots 0, 5, 10, and 15, leaving four blanks between each. Fill the blanks so the whole line reads like an ordinary note: each secret letter must start a real word.

Part B, prove it: run Drill 1's extractor on your cover and show it yields your secret exactly.

Part C, the exchange: leave your innocent note where a partner will find it. They extract; a bystander reads the same slip and sees nothing. Stretch: encrypt the secret with a Day 2 wheel first, hide the coded version, and hand your partner the key separately.

cover = "SEND MORE OATS SOON "
secret = ""
i = 0
for letter in cover:
    if i % 5 == 0:
        secret = secret + letter
    i = i + 1
print(secret)
Slots 0, 5, 10, 15 hold S, M, O, S: the extractor prints SMOS... which is why this sample is a failed cover, left here on purpose. The worksheet order matters: secret letters first, filler second. A cover written the other way around smuggles whatever it happens to smuggle.

Standards, pacing & evidence (teacher panel)

Where this fits: thirty-five minutes, terminal only, the Wing's lightest mission and a strong first pick for recruits intimidated by the Wheel: one gate, one counter, no cipher machinery. The concept is real and current, from microdots to pixels; the reveal keeps it at the acrostic level. Drill 3's capacity arithmetic and the failed sample in the build are the two deliberate teeth: cover-writing is a planning problem, and the sample models the failure so recruits meet it on the page instead of in their own work.

Where recruits actually stall: counting from 1. Every channel position in this mission starts at 0, and the hinge's Part 1 is built to catch the off-by-one. In the build, recruits who fill the blanks first and place secret letters second produce accidental secrets; the reveal's failed sample exists so you can point at it rather than at a recruit. The Part C bystander should be a genuine third party for the payoff to land.
