Drill 1 · The three-part debriefE5-ALG-PS-01

Two minutes, in this order

The debrief order (1) Run. Start your gadget from a fresh start and let it run while you talk. (2) Point. On your signed schematic, show where the watch loop turns, where the wrap decision and the respawn decision sit, and where your alert gate and your own routine fire. (3) Tell. Name one design choice you made and why, then read one row of your Bug Hunt Log aloud: the symptom, and the one change that fixed it.

A gadget that crashed on Thursday and runs on Friday is the better story. The log proves you can hunt bugs; read from it proudly. Rehearse once with a partner before your turn: the partner times you and holds up a hand at two minutes.

Predict Before you rehearse: the respawn decision and the wrap decision both live inside the ring, but one of them sits inside a second loop. Which one, and where on your schematic will your finger land when you point to it?
The respawn decision sits inside the beacon loop, which sits inside the ring: your finger lands two layers deep, on the loop-inside-the-loop. The wrap decision sits one layer deep, in the ring but outside the beacon loop. Thursday's hinge counted them: 12 respawn checks per pass, one wrap check per pass. If your schematic shows both decisions side by side at the same depth, redraw that corner before your turn; it's the one place the schematic most often lies.
Drill 2 · The audience's what-ifE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

One question per agent, answered by prediction

After each debrief, the audience asks exactly one question, and it has to be a what-if about the Ops Panel or a gate: "What happens if you set DRIFT_SPEED to 0?" The presenter predicts aloud. Nobody runs the change; the prediction is the answer, the way it has been all week. Try the format on the clean engine first.

WHAT-IF: "What happens if DRIFT_SPEED = 0?"
Predict Answer it as a presenter would, in one breath: what stops, what keeps happening, and which line decides each.
The beacons stop rising, because ys[i] = ys[i] - DRIFT_SPEED now subtracts nothing. Everything else keeps going: the wobble still nudges x (the Dispatcher's line reads no constant), the batteries still drain and the beacons still shrink and respawn (the drain line has its own constant, DRAIN_RATE), and the sweep still crosses and wraps. A good what-if answer names the line, not just the effect. Good audience questions for today: the sweep at speed 0, the alert gate at frame 0, BEACON_COUNT at 1, FULL_BATTERY at 10.
While you wait your turn · The Dead Drop exchange

Send a coded message across the room

Day 2's Cipher Wheel, one more time. Encode a short message for an agent across the room with a shift of your choosing, write the coded text on a slip, and pass it. Crack whatever lands on your desk with Day 2's Drill 11 and write the shift on the back. Quietly: presentations are happening.

Exchange rules Capitals and spaces only, no names, no shift written on the slip. If a slip cracks on no line at all, the sender used a character that isn't on the 27-slot rack; send it back with "unknown character" written on it, which is Day 2's Drill 2 coming true.

Choose your exit gate

Floor · approaching the standard

You run your gadget for the room (the clean engine plus your one change counts) and point to the ring on your signed schematic.

Middle · meets the standard

All three parts, in order, inside two minutes: the gadget runs, both decisions and your own routine are pointed to on the schematic, one design choice is told, and one log row is read aloud with all four columns.

Stretch · exceeds the standard

You answer the audience's what-if by naming the exact line that decides it, and you crack at least one dead drop before your turn comes.

The badge ceremony · closing block

Field Agent, commissioned

After the last debrief, badges. Each agent is called by codename, and the Director reads one line from their schematic or their log. Take the Field Notebook home: every trace in it is something you can now do without the terminal, which is the whole point of the Academy.

Standards, pacing & evidence (teacher panel)

The pacing choice, stated plainly: Day 5 is presentations, and nothing else is scheduled. The Bug Hunt that a five-day week would normally put here was cut on purpose: debugging moved into Day 4's Commission block with the protocol and the log, and the capstone deadline became finished rather than running. The two drills on this page are rehearsal devices, not content: the first makes recruits locate both decisions on their own schematic before they point at them in public, and the second turns the audience into a predict-before-reveal instrument.

Time plan: two and a half minutes per agent including the what-if, so fifty minutes for twenty agents; ten minutes of setup so every gadget starts fresh and every window is visible; ten minutes for badges. The Dead Drop exchange absorbs every minute in between and needs no teaching. If the day is shorter than that, cut the what-if first and the exchange second; never cut Run, Point, Tell.
