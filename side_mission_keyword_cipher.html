The Keyword Cipher

The Cipher Grid's Atbash rack was fixed: every agent's reversed alphabet is the same reversed alphabet. This machine builds a personal coded rack from a keyword you can carry in your head: FALCON first, then everything unused. One memorized word, a whole substitution alphabet. Then you'll find the machine's lazy tail, because every cipher in this wing gets audited.

Drill 1 · Building the rackE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

The keyword first, then everyone still waiting

Seat the keyword's letters first, then walk the plain rack and seat everyone still waiting. Both passes use the same doorman: the Safecracker's mole-hunt search, admitting a character only if it isn't already seated. The result is a full 27-slot rack, no repeats, no absences, even if the keyword itself stutters.

rack = "ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ "
keyword = "FALCON"
coded = ""
for letter in keyword:
    used = 0
    for j in range(len(coded)):
        if coded[j] == letter:
            used = 1
    if used == 0:
        coded = coded + letter
for letter in rack:
    used = 0
    for j in range(len(coded)):
        if coded[j] == letter:
            used = 1
    if used == 0:
        coded = coded + letter
print(coded)
print(len(coded))
Predict Which is the first plain-rack letter the used-check skips? Which letter sits in slot 6, right after the keyword? And why must the final length be exactly 27?
A is the first skip: the walk meets it immediately and finds it already seated inside FALCON. Slot 6 holds B, the first survivor after the keyword's six. And the length must be 27 because the builder is a seating chart, not a filter: every character gets exactly one chair, keyword letters up front, everyone else in their old order behind. If your length prints 26, a keyword letter sneaked in twice.

Drill 2 · The swap machine rides againE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Encode on one rack, decode on the other

From here it is the Cipher Grid's machine exactly: search the plain rack, emit from the coded rack; decode by swapping their jobs. New alphabet, old engine.

rack = "ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ "
coded = "FALCONBDEGHIJKMPQRSTUVWXYZ "
message = "STRIKE AT NOON"
result = ""
for letter in message:
    slot = 0
    for i in range(27):
        if rack[i] == letter:
            slot = i
    result = result + coded[slot]
print(result)
Predict Trace S, T, R through the racks. Something odd happens. What comes out, and can you see why before the reveal?
S, T, and R come out as S, T, and R. Slots 17 through 19 hold the same letters on both racks, so those letters walk through the cipher untouched. The message's opening word barely changes at all, and if that feels like a broken cipher, good instinct: it isn't broken, it's lazy, and the next drill measures exactly how lazy.

Drill 3 · The lazy tailE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

Audit: count the letters that don't move

Walk both racks in step and count every slot where they agree. Each agreement is a letter your cipher leaves in plain sight.

rack = "ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ "
coded = "FALCONBDEGHIJKMPQRSTUVWXYZ "
lazy = ""
for i in range(27):
    if rack[i] == coded[i]:
        lazy = lazy + rack[i]
print("Unmoved:", lazy)
print("Count:", len(lazy))
Predict FALCON is six letters, and it only rearranges the front of the alphabet. Roughly how much of the rack do you expect to survive unmoved, and will the space move?
Twelve characters, P through Z and the space: nearly half the rack encodes as itself. The keyword shuffles only the seats in front of it; once the removed letters are all spent, the tail realigns and everything from P on sits back in its own chair. The space staying put is the worst leak of all, the Cipher Room's C-giveaway in reverse. The repair is keyword choice, and the build makes you prove yours is better.

Hinge question · stop here until you're sureE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Choosing a keyword like a professional

Part 1: the keyword is BALLOON. The builder's used-check meets the second L and the second O. What does it do, and how long is the finished rack?
Part 2: which keyword leaves a shorter lazy tail, FALCON or ZEBRA QUILT? Reason it out before you test it.

Part 1: the doorman skips both twins, because each repeated letter is already seated by the time its double arrives, and the rack still finishes at exactly 27: BALON up front, then the survivors. That first pass is the reason the builder walks the keyword through the same used-check as everyone else; a builder that seated the keyword blindly would give BALLOON a 29-chair rack with two Ls answering to one slot. Part 2: ZEBRA QUILT, and the reasoning is the audit's lesson: a tail realigns only after the last removed letter's old position is passed, so keywords carrying late letters (Z, the space, U, T) keep the shuffle alive deep into the rack. FALCON's letters all live in the front half, which is why its tail slept.
Field Mission · Your Personal Alphabet

Build it, audit it, trade it

Part A, build: your own keyword through Drill 1's builder. Confirm 27.

Part B, audit: Drill 3's counter on your rack. Write the unmoved count in your notebook next to the keyword.

Part C, the trade: encode a message on your rack and pass the coded text to a classmate with the keyword spoken, never written: they rebuild your entire rack from one remembered word and decode by rack swap. That rebuild-from-a-word is the machine's whole reason to exist.

Stretch: hunt for a keyword whose unmoved count is 3 or less, and explain what your winner has that FALCON lacked.

Standards, pacing & evidence (teacher panel)

Where this fits: thirty-five minutes, terminal only, sequenced after the Cipher Grid whose rack-swap engine it reuses wholesale; the only new construction is the builder loop, which is the Safecracker's mole hunt repurposed. The mission's spine is the audit: recruits build a cipher, then measure it, then improve it by reasoned keyword choice. That build-measure-improve loop is worth more than the cipher.

Where recruits actually stall: the builder's two-loop nesting reads dense on first contact; the seating-chart metaphor in Drill 1's reveal unsticks most, and a paper walk of the first five plain letters unsticks the rest. In Part C, the spoken keyword gets misheard exactly as it does in the Wheel mission, and the resulting garbage decode is a fine argument for the Tamper Seal. The stretch hunt turns competitive fast; a leaderboard of unmoved counts on the board costs nothing.
