The Shared Secret

Every mission so far has the same soft spot: the key has to travel. Spoken keywords, seed numbers, pad booklets, all of it can be overheard. In 1976 three mathematicians found the impossible-sounding fix: two agents agree on a secret number while the enemy hears every word they say. The whole internet runs on it tonight. You will run it across a classroom.

The paint story · read this first

Two agents, one loud room

Imagine mixing paint instead of numbers. The room agrees, out loud, on a common yellow. You secretly pick red and mix yellow-red; your partner secretly picks blue and mixes yellow-blue. You trade the mixtures in the open, and each of you stirs your own secret color into what arrived: you both now hold yellow-red-blue, the identical muddy shade. The eavesdropper holds yellow, saw both mixtures pass, and is stuck, because paint is easy to mix and nearly impossible to unmix. The drills below do this with numbers, where "mix" is multiply-and-wrap, and unmixing is a search that grows monstrous as the numbers grow.

Drill 1 · The tower of powersE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Multiply, wrap, repeat

The mixing machine: start at 1, multiply by the public base 5, wrap with % 23, and repeat your secret number of times. The room's public numbers are 23 and 5; your secret is how many turns of the crank, here 6.

value = 1
for i in range(6):
    value = value * 5 % 23
    print(value)
Predict Walk the first three passes by hand: 1 times 5, then times 5 again, wrapped at 23 each time. And the important question: why does the % happen inside the loop instead of once at the end?
5, then 25 wrapping to 2, then 10, and the tower climbs 4, 20, 8. Wrapping inside the loop is what keeps the machine runnable: without it, six turns of the crank is 15625, and fifteen turns is over thirty billion, a number your Drill 9 while would grind against. Wrapped every pass, the value never leaves the small circle of 0 to 22, yet lands exactly where the giant number would have landed. That one trick, wrap as you go, is what lets real computers raise numbers to exponents with hundreds of digits.

Drill 2 · The tradeE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Two towers, spoken aloud

You crank 6 times and get 8. Your partner secretly cranks 15 times. Both results get said out loud across the room, enemy listening.

mine = 1
for i in range(6):
    mine = mine * 5 % 23
theirs = 1
for i in range(15):
    theirs = theirs * 5 % 23
print("I announce:", mine)
print("They announce:", theirs)
Predict Your announcement is 8. Will the partner's 15-crank tower land higher than yours, lower, or is there no telling? What does the eavesdropper now hold?
There is no telling from the outside: the wrap scrambles all sense of higher and lower, and 15 cranks land on 19. The eavesdropper now holds four numbers: 23, 5, 8, and 19, everything ever said. What the eavesdropper does not hold is either crank count, and the crank counts never crossed the room. Keep that inventory in your notebook; the hinge asks about it.

Drill 3 · The meetingE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Stir your secret into what arrived

The finish: take the number your partner announced and crank it by your secret count. They do the same with yours. Two different roads, and the paint story promises one destination.

mine = 1
for i in range(6):
    mine = mine * 19 % 23
theirs = 1
for i in range(15):
    theirs = theirs * 8 % 23
print("My secret meeting point:", mine)
print("Their secret meeting point:", theirs)
Predict You crank their 19 six times; they crank your 8 fifteen times. Different starting numbers, different crank counts. Will the two meeting points match?
Both land on 2. Six cranks of base 5 then fifteen more is twenty-one cranks; fifteen then six is also twenty-one; the order of stirring never mattered, exactly like the paint. The number 2 was never spoken, never written, never sent, and both agents now hold it. That is the entire miracle, and it is not a trick: it is multiplication not caring about order, wearing a wrap.

Drill 4 · The eavesdropper's problemE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

Crack it yourself, then price the real thing

Be the enemy. You heard 23, 5, and the announcement 8. Find the secret crank count by brute force: the Safecracker's count-up, aimed at a tower.

target = 8
for guess in range(1, 23):
    value = 1
    for i in range(guess):
        value = value * 5 % 23
    if value == target:
        print("The secret crank count is", guess)
Predict Will this find the secret? So the toy exchange is crackable. Real systems use a wheel with hundreds of digits instead of 23. What happens to this exact loop then?
It prints 6 almost instantly, and your classroom exchange lies broken, which is the honest half of the lesson. The other half: this loop's work grows with the wheel, and at real sizes the count-up outlives the sun, the earth, and every computer ever built running together. Growing the wheel costs the agents almost nothing (the wrap keeps their numbers small) and costs the eavesdropper everything. Same asymmetry as the Safecracker's extra digit, raised to a power that ate the whole problem. That asymmetry is modern cryptography.

The eavesdropper's inventory

Part 1: fifteen cranks of 5 without any wrapping is 30517578125. Your Drill 2 loop reached the same destination without its value ever leaving 0 to 22. Which line made that possible, and where exactly does it sit?
Part 2: list everything the eavesdropper heard in Drills 2 and 3, then name the two things that never crossed the room.

Part 1: value = value * 5 % 23, inside the loop, wrapping on every pass; moved outside, the loop rebuilds the thirty-billion tower first and wraps once at the top, same answer bought at a price no real-sized wheel could pay. Part 2: the room heard 23, 5, 8, and 19, and never heard 6 or 15, the two crank counts. The meeting point, 2, follows only from a crank count plus an announcement, so the eavesdropper, holding announcements alone, stands outside a door with no handle. Say that inventory aloud at the debrief; it is the whole proof in one breath.
Field Mission · Agree in the Open

A secret built by shouting

Part A, the exchange: partner up across the room. Agree out loud: wheel 23, base 5. Each pick a secret crank count from 2 to 20, tell no one, crank, and announce your tower's landing. Each crank the other's announcement by your own secret. Compare meeting points by whisper: they match, and the room heard everything except how.

Part B, spend it: your meeting point % 27 is now a Caesar key you never exchanged. Encode a message with the Day 2 wheel and pass only the coded text; your partner already holds the key.

Part C, stretch: play the eavesdropper on another pair: their four public numbers are enough for Drill 4's cracker. Crack their crank counts, decode their traffic, then tell them why the real internet survives you.

rack = "ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ "
shift = 2 % 27
message = "WELL DONE AGENT"
result = ""
for letter in message:
    slot = 0
    for i in range(27):
        if rack[i] == letter:
            slot = i
    result = result + rack[(slot + shift) % 27]
print(result)
The meeting point 2 wraps to key 2 on the 27-wheel, and decoding is the complement, 25, exactly as Day 2 taught. Two missions, chained into a working cryptosystem: agree in the open, then lock with what you agreed.

Standards, pacing & evidence (teacher panel)

Where this fits: fifty minutes, terminal only, the Wing's summit; sequence it last. It is Diffie-Hellman key exchange at classroom scale, and everything in it is owned machinery: the tower is a tally that multiplies, the wrap is Day 2's %, the cracker is the Safecracker's count-up. The paint story carries the intuition and the drills carry the arithmetic; run the story as theater before anyone opens a laptop. The honesty beat matters: Drill 4 cracks the toy on purpose, and the mission's last word is the asymmetry, not an unearned "unbreakable."

Where recruits actually stall: Drill 3's why-do-they-match is the room's argument; the twenty-one-cranks-either-way sentence settles it, and writing 6 + 15 and 15 + 6 on the board settles it faster. In the build, partners who pick crank count 1 announce the base itself and get teased by the eavesdroppers, which is its own lesson. Part B's key is tiny; a pair whose meeting point wraps to 0 has built the shift that changes nothing, and should laugh, pick new secrets, and go again.
