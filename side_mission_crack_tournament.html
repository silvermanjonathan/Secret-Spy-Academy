The Crack Tournament

Everything the Wing taught, turned into a contest. Teams forge drops, teams crack drops, and the scoreboard rewards the clever way over the patient way. This page is the event kit: the laws, the leagues, the referee's checker, and the score sheet. No new code lives here; the weapons were issued in the missions.

Forge · Crack · Score

YOU'LL NEED: the class in teams of two or three · every team's Day 2 machines working · slips of paper · a referee with this page open

Tournament law · the drop format

Every drop is a slip bearing coded text only. The plain message must be capitals and spaces, at least 20 characters, and real words. Shift League keys run 1 to 26; no key 0, no key 27, and the maker writes the key on a second slip that goes face-down to the referee before the round starts. A drop whose key slip is wrong scores zero for its maker. Referees rule; rulings are final; good sports crack again next round.

The two leagues

Shift League (everyone): drops are Caesar-wheel drops from Day 2. Crackers may use any method: the clever way (count the top letter, subtract from E's slot, name the key) or the patient way (the 27-key parade, find the readable line).

Armored League (Wheel graduates): drops are Vigenère drops with keywords of exactly three letters, keyword on the face-down slip. Crackers may use anything from the Wing. Fair warning from the Wheel's Drill 3: the top-letter trick lies here, and knowing that it lies is worth points.

The referee's checker

When a team claims a crack, the referee runs the claim: paste the drop's coded text and the claimed key, and the checker decodes with the complement. A readable message settles it in the claimer's favor.

rack = "ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ "
coded = "DSPKFLEWDKTCKPX DI"
claimed_key = 11
result = ""
for letter in coded:
    slot = 0
    for i in range(27):
        if rack[i] == letter:
            slot = i
    result = result + rack[(slot + 27 - claimed_key) % 27]
print(result)
The sample drop opens clean, so the claim stands and the points are awarded. For Armored League claims, the referee swaps in the Wheel's Drill 2 decoder and the claimed keyword. The checker exists so no argument lasts longer than one run: in this room, the terminal is the referee's whistle.

Roles & rounds

Quartermaster (one per team): runs the encoder, guards the key slip, delivers the drop. Cracker (the rest of the team): runs the counts and parades. Rotate roles every round so every recruit forges and cracks at least once.

A round: five minutes to forge, referee collects key slips, drops go up on the board, ten minutes to crack anything on the board except your own, claims to the referee as they land. Three rounds makes a tournament; the third round is traditionally Armored League only, floor teams continuing in Shift League with honor intact.

Standards, pacing & evidence (teacher panel)

Where this fits: forty-five to sixty minutes, the Wing's closing event, any time after Day 2; the Armored League needs at least two teams who finished the Unbreakable Wheel. It is deliberately a page of laws rather than drills: assessment disguised as a tournament, with the scoring rubric doing the differentiation. The 3-versus-1 split between clever and patient cracks pays understanding over persistence without punishing persistence, and the calling-a-lie point exists so Armored-drop failures become knowledge instead of embarrassment.

Where the event actually stalls: key slips. Collect them before the round or claims become unadjudicable, which is why the wrong-key-slip zero is written into law. Teams who crack their own drop for easy points are met by the not-your-own rule. If one team dominates, the traditional handicap is making them announce which method they'll use before touching the drop. Keep the checker open the whole time; the terminal-as-whistle line is the classroom-management trick of the day.

Deliberately not covered here: new mechanics of any kind. Prize structures are left to the room, with one house recommendation: the trophy names a team, never a fastest individual, because the quartermaster who forged the unbroken drop earned it sitting still. Keys 0 and 27 are outlawed not as trivia but because both encode nothing, and a nothing-drop wastes a round.
