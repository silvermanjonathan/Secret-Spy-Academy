Drill 1 · Read the Ops PanelE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

Every constant is a promise

The panel is the engine's contract. Before touching anything, say what each constant controls. This is Day 3's reading, aimed at today's changes.

BEACON_COLORS = [(123, 228, 149), (242, 233, 211), (201, 139, 31)]
BEACON_COUNT = 12
DRIFT_SPEED = 1
SWEEP_SPEED = 4
DRAIN_RATE = 1
FULL_BATTERY = 200

SCOPE_LEFT = 120
SCOPE_TOP = 40
SCOPE_SIZE = 400
CENTER_X = 320
CENTER_Y = 240
INK = (27, 31, 38)
PHOSPHOR = (123, 228, 149)
Predict Which constant changes how crowded the scope is, and which changes how fast beacons rise? Which one makes beacons die sooner if you raise it? The sweep takes 101 passes to cross the scope at speed 4: what would SWEEP_SPEED = 8 do to that number?
BEACON_COUNT sets the crowd, DRIFT_SPEED the rise, and DRAIN_RATE the dying: raise it to 2 and a full battery lasts 100 passes instead of 200. At speed 8 the sweep crosses in 51 passes, about half. The scope's center lives at (CENTER_X, CENTER_Y), Day 3's coordinates, and the panel has no length constant: the run lasts until the agent closes the window, and the frame briefcase counts upward inside it so timed gates can fire. If you can predict a constant's effect, you have permission to change it. That is the whole test.
Drill 2 · The Gadget SchematicE5-ALG-PS-01

Draw the engine before you bend it

On your schematic sheet, draw the engine as boxes and arrows: setup at the top, the watched loop as a ring, and inside the ring the beats: move the sweep (with its wrap decision), update the beacons (with the respawn decision), draw. Label where BEACON_COUNT, SWEEP_SPEED, and batteries[i] live. Your teacher signs it. Only then do you customize.

Predict Before drawing: how many flat checkpoints live inside the ring, and which beat holds each? Where on your schematic will your own changes land: inside the ring, in the panel, or both?
Two checkpoints: the wrap decision in the sweep beat, and the respawn decision in the beacon beat, which sits inside the beacon loop, which sits inside the ring. And your Commission lands in both places: the color list, count, and speeds live in the panel; the alert-mode gate and your own gadget fire inside the ring. A plan that shows where each change lives is what turns customizing from guessing into engineering, and it's the artifact this page exists to collect.
Drill 3 · Worked change I: alert modeE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

A decision inside the ring

Day 1's flat checkpoint, reading Day 2's frame tally. Inside the watch loop, find the sweep's pygame.draw.line call (the two lines ending in 3)) and replace them with these five. Predict, then run.

    sweep_color = PHOSPHOR                     # the default
    if frame > 600:                            # alert mode: ten seconds in
        sweep_color = (201, 139, 31)
    pygame.draw.line(screen, sweep_color,
                     (sweep_x, SCOPE_TOP), (sweep_x, SCOPE_BOTTOM), 3)
Predict At 60 passes per second, how many seconds into the run does alert mode begin? What would frame > 900 do instead? And why does the gate have no else?
Ten seconds in: pass 600 at 60 passes per second. Move the gate to 900 and alert mode begins at fifteen seconds instead. No else is needed because the line above the gate already set the default; the gate only overrides it. One flat checkpoint reading the frame briefcase, and your gadget has an act two. Mark on your schematic where it fires.

Drill 4 · Worked change II: your own gadgetE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

A def in the blueprint, a call in the ring

Yesterday you wrote draw_beacon. The same move gives the scope a lock ring: define it right below draw_scope, and call it right after draw_scope(screen) inside the loop.

def draw_lock_ring(surface):
    pygame.draw.circle(surface, (201, 139, 31), (CENTER_X, CENTER_Y), 24, 2)

# ...inside the watch loop, right after draw_scope(screen):
    draw_lock_ring(screen)
Predict What will the ring look like, and where? What would happen if the call were placed above screen.fill(INK) instead of below it?
A thin amber ring, radius 24, sitting on the crosshair at the scope's center. Placed above the fill, it would be drawn and then painted over by the ink on the very same pass, so you'd never see it: inside the ring, order is everything. Your own gadget should be yours: a ring, a row of circles, a box, whatever you can draw with circles, lines, and rects. Update the schematic to show where it fires.

while the sweep passes through it.
Hinge question · stop here until you're sureE5-ALG-PS-01

The count inside the ring

Look at your schematic. The respawn decision sits inside the beacon loop, which sits inside the ring. The wrap decision sits in the ring, outside the beacon loop.
Part 1: in one pass, how many times does the respawn decision run? And across the first ten seconds of a run, 600 passes?
Part 2: in those same 600 passes, how many times does the wrap decision run?

Part 1: 12 times per pass, once per beacon, and 7,200 times in those ten seconds (12 × 600). Part 2: 600 times, once per pass. If you said once for Part 1, your schematic is missing the loop inside the ring; redraw before the Commission. If you got both, you can see the whole machine at once, which is exactly what the schematic is for.
The Commission · due before you leave todayE5-ALG-PS-01

Your Commissioned Gadget: a customized Tracker Scope

Deadline law Your gadget is finished, running, and rehearsed by the end of Day 4. Tomorrow is the Final Debrief: you present; nothing gets fixed tomorrow. Worst case at day's end, revert to the clean engine plus your one safest change, and present that. Nobody walks into Day 5 with nothing running.

The four requirements. Each is a tool from this week, and house law holds: one change per run, predict before each.

1. Your colors. Rebuild BEACON_COLORS as your own list of 3–5 color tuples.
2. Your numbers. Change BEACON_COUNT and one speed (SWEEP_SPEED or DRIFT_SPEED); predict each effect before the run.
3. Your alert mode. Drill 3's gate, at a frame you choose and can defend.
4. Your own gadget. Your own def, your own drawing, called in the ring, marked on the schematic.

When it misbehaves: the Bug Hunt protocol Symptom (what actually happened) → Predict the cause → Trace to the exact line → One change → Re-run. Every bug gets a row in the Bug Hunt Log in your Field Notebook: Symptom | Predicted cause | Line found | The one change. A loud bug hands you a traceback with the line number in it; read the last line first, the way you did on Day 1. A silent bug has no traceback: the gadget runs and does the wrong thing, and the only way to catch it is to compare what it does against what your schematic says it should do. Two silent bugs to expect today: a gate that never fires because the frame number is higher than the run ever reaches, and a routine that draws before the fill and gets painted over.

Before you leave: the gadget runs clean from a fresh start, the schematic is signed and matches it, the log holds at least one real row, and you have rehearsed the three-part Debrief once with a partner: Run it, Point at the ring and the decisions on your schematic, Tell one design choice and read one log row aloud.

The clean engine runs, plus one predicted change from the Ops Panel, and your schematic shows the setup and the ring, teacher-signed.

Middle · meets the standard

Your signed schematic shows sequence, the ring, both decisions, and your labeled variables, and your running gadget matches it: your color list, your numbers, and the alert-mode gate all wired in. Your Bug Hunt Log holds at least one real row from your own code, all four columns filled.

Stretch · exceeds the standard

Your own def gadget fires in the ring and lives on the schematic, and you can point to the exact pass number when your gate flips and say why. Optional final flourish: grow the alert gate into a ladder with a stand-down rung, a second pass number you choose and can defend where the sweep turns green again.

Standards, pacing & evidence (teacher panel)

The pacing choice, stated plainly: no new syntax today, and that is the proudest line on the page. Everything the Commission asks for was owned at least a day ago: the panel constants are Day 1's briefcases, the alert gate is Day 1's flat checkpoint reading Day 3's frame tally, and the lock ring is Day 3's def with Day 3's drawing vocabulary. Load stays level because integration replaces novelty. Day 4 is also where debugging is collected: Day 5 is presentations only, so the Commission block carries the Bug Hunt protocol and the log, and the deadline is finished, not merely running.

Where recruits actually stall: Block A (Drills 1 and 2, about 35 minutes) is the schematic; the stall is drawing the beacon loop as a box instead of a loop inside the ring, which the hinge is built to catch. Sign schematics as they come, not at the end. Block B (Drills 3 and 4 plus the hinge, about 40 minutes): the common Drill 3 error is replacing only one of the two draw.line lines; the common Drill 4 error is calling the ring before the fill. Block C, the Commission, the bug hunting, and the rehearsal, needs everything that is left, at least 60 minutes; call a hard stop fifteen minutes before the bell for the rehearsal. The deadline law is real: a recruit with nothing running at the bell reverts to the clean engine plus one change, and that counts. Collect the log rows as recruits finish; the two planted-by-nature silent bugs (a gate set past the run's length, a routine drawn before the fill) appear in most rooms and are worth naming aloud. For the stretch gate's question: the sweep wraps every 101 passes, so it wraps five times in the first 600.

Deliberately not covered here: no new syntax at all; this page spends everything on planning, predicted change, and repair, building directly on Day 3's reading. The VS Code debugger is not taught: a breakpoint ritual (red dot in the gutter, Start Debugging, predict the variable, then look) is a fine one-on-one tool for a recruit stuck on a silent bug, but there is no room for it as a drill. return values remain excluded: every routine acts by drawing or printing, and recruits who reach for return get one-on-one support, by program-wide design. The run is Day 2's watched loop and ends only when the agent closes the window; pygame.event.pump and pygame.time.wait appear nowhere in this Academy's engines, because every window reads its mail. The frame tally keeps time inside the watch so gates can fire at chosen moments. Sprites, image files, and classes are excluded program-wide.
