Drill 1 · Read the Ops PanelE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

Every knob is a promise

The panel is the game's contract, and today it is your design surface. Before touching anything, say what each knob controls. This is Day 3's reading, aimed at today's changes.

# ---------- THE OPS PANEL (your customizations live here) ----------
LOCK_SPEED = 5             # how far the arrow keys move the lock each pass
TARGET_DARTS = [-2, -1, -1, 0, 0, 1, 1, 2]  # the target's possible darts; repeat a number to make it likelier
TURN_EVERY = 25            # the target picks a new direction every 25 passes
LOCK_RANGE = 26            # how close counts as on target (the dot inside the ring)
POINT_EVERY = 50           # hold the lock this many passes in a row to score
WIN_SCORE = 10             # first to this many points wins; keep it 28 or fewer so the pips fit

SCOPE_LEFT = 120
SCOPE_TOP = 40
SCOPE_SIZE = 400
CENTER_X = 320
CENTER_Y = 240
INK = (27, 31, 38)
PHOSPHOR = (123, 228, 149)
AMBER = (201, 139, 31)
CORAL = (255, 120, 90)
# -------------------------------------------------------------------
Predict At LOCK_SPEED 5, how far does one full second of held RIGHT carry the lock? Which knob makes each point take longer to earn, and which makes the target jumpier? What would LOCK_RANGE 30 feel like, and LOCK_RANGE 18?
Three hundred pixels: 60 passes of plus 5, most of the scope in one second. No wonder the lock feels quick. POINT_EVERY sets the price of a point, TURN_EVERY sets the jumpiness (lower is jumpier), and LOCK_RANGE is the forgiveness dial: 30 reads as generous, 18 as cruel, because the box test opens only inside that distance on both axes. The dice rack is the target's whole personality: more 0s and it lies low, an extra 3 and -3 and it dashes. If you can predict a knob's effect, you have permission to change it. That is the test.
Drill 2 · The Gadget SchematicE5-ALG-PS-01

Draw the game before you bend it

On your schematic sheet, draw the game as boxes and arrows: setup at the top, the watched loop as a ring, and inside the ring the beats in order: listen (the QUIT decision), steer (four key gates and the fence), move the target (the timed reroll decision and the four walls), the lock test (four checkpoints and the verdict), score (the streak gate and the winning condition), draw. Label where LOCK_RANGE, streak, and TARGET_DARTS live. Your teacher signs it. Only then do you customize.

Predict Before drawing: which beat holds the most checkpoints, and how many? Where on your schematic will your own changes land: inside the ring, in the panel, or both?
The lock test: four checkpoints feeding the counter, plus the verdict gate that reads it, five decisions in one beat. It earns its own box. And your Commission lands in both places: the difficulty, the colors, and the banner racks live in the panel and the setup; your own gadget fires inside the ring. A plan that shows where each change lives is what turns customizing from guessing into engineering, and it's the artifact this page exists to collect.
Drill 3 · Points and the winE5-PRO-RD-?? ⚠

The streak, the score, and the stand-down

Hold the lock for POINT_EVERY passes in a row and the streak gate fires: a point, a pip, a terminal line. Reach WIN_SCORE and the winning condition flips mission_complete; from the next pass, one flat gate zeroes the target's dart and another zeroes the streak, so the world freezes and the score stays put. The window stays open until you close it, by house law.

    if streak == POINT_EVERY:
        score = score + 1
        streak = 0
        print(f"Point! Score: {score} of {WIN_SCORE}")
    if score == WIN_SCORE:                      # the winning condition
        mission_complete = True
Predict In a full game, exactly how many Point lines print, and what does the last one say? On the pass after the tenth point, what does the target do, and why doesn't the window close?
Exactly ten lines, and the last says Point! Score: 10 of 10. The next pass, the stand-down gate zeroes the dart and the target freezes where it was caught; the streak gate is starved, so no eleventh point can ever print. The window stays open because no window in this Academy closes itself: you close it, and the terminal answers with MISSION COMPLETE: 10 points. and your on-target count.

Drill 4 · The victory bannerE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

Words in the window, without breaking the law

The law says words live in the terminal, and pygame.font is banned all week. So how does MISSION FINISHED get on screen? The same way everything else does: a rack, a loop, a gate, a rectangle. Each row of the banner is a string; every X in it becomes one small square, placed by the pips' rule.

MISSION_ROWS = [
"X   X XXXXX  XXXX  XXXX XXXXX  XXX  X   X",
"XX XX   X   X     X       X   X   X XX  X",
"X X X   X    XXX   XXX    X   X   X X X X",
"X   X   X       X     X   X   X   X X  XX",
"X   X XXXXX XXXX  XXXX  XXXXX  XXX  X   X",
]

def draw_banner(surface, rows, left, top):
    for r in range(5):
        row = rows[r]
        for c in range(len(row)):
            if row[c] == "X":
                pygame.draw.rect(surface, PHOSPHOR,
                                 (left + c * 8, top + r * 8, 7, 7))
Predict Count the Xs in the letter I alone (columns 7 to 11 of the rack). How many squares is it drawn from? And if you wanted the banner to say something else, which part of the program would you edit, and which part would you leave alone?
Thirteen: five across the top, five across the bottom, and three down the middle. To change the message you edit only the racks, drawing your letters in Xs; draw_banner never changes, because the routine holds the how and the rack holds the what, Day 3's lesson in one def. Keep each row 48 characters or fewer so it fits the scope, and recenter with left = 320 minus 4 times the row's length.
Hinge question · stop here until you're sureE5-ALG-PS-01

The count inside the ring

Look at your schematic.
Part 1: in one pass, how many times does the lock test's counter line close = close + 1 run at most, and how many times does the streak gate run?
Part 2: across the first ten seconds of a run, 600 passes, what is the greatest number of points a perfect agent could earn at POINT_EVERY 50, and why?

Part 1: at most 4 times, once per checkpoint, and the streak gate runs exactly once per pass. If your schematic shows the four checkpoints outside the ring, redraw before the Commission. Part 2: twelve points, because every point costs fifty held passes and 600 passes hold at most twelve fifties. A perfect agent's score is rationed by the ring itself. You can read that off the schematic without running a thing.
The Commission · finished before you leave todayE5-ALG-PS-01

Your Commissioned Edition of Target Lock

Deadline law Your edition is finished, running, and rehearsed by the end of Day 4. Tomorrow is the Final Debrief: you present; nothing gets fixed tomorrow. Worst case at day's end, revert to the clean game plus your one safest change, and present that. Nobody walks into Day 5 with nothing running.

The four requirements. Each is a tool from this week, and house law holds: one change per run, predict before each.

1. Your difficulty. Design a kid mode and a pro mode: two full Ops Panels written in your Field Notebook, every knob predicted in one sentence before its run. The dials, and which way each turns the game:

2. Your colors. Rebuild the palette: your own ink, crosshair, and target tuples.
3. Your banner. The victory message in your own words, drawn in Xs on the racks, every row 48 characters or fewer, recentered with left = 320 minus 4 times the row's length.
4. Your own gadget. Your own def, your own drawing, called in the ring, marked on the schematic: a second range ring, corner brackets on the lock, a border that changes with the score, whatever you can draw with circles, lines, and rects.

When it misbehaves: the Bug Hunt protocol Symptom (what actually happened) → Predict the cause → Trace to the exact line → One change → Re-run. Every bug gets a row in the Bug Hunt Log in your Field Notebook: Symptom | Predicted cause | Line found | The one change. A loud bug hands you a traceback with the line number in it; read the last line first, the way you did on Day 1. A silent bug has no traceback: the game runs and does the wrong thing, and the only way to catch it is to compare what it does against what your schematic says it should do. Two silent bugs to expect today: a LOCK_RANGE cut so small the crosshair never reads green, and a banner row edited to a new length but never recentered, so the message leans out of the frame.

Before you leave: your edition runs clean from a fresh start, the schematic is signed and matches it, the log holds at least one real row, and you have rehearsed the three-part Debrief once with a partner: Run it, Point at the ring and the decisions on your schematic, Tell one design choice and read one log row aloud.

Standards, pacing & evidence (teacher panel)

The pacing choice, stated plainly: no new syntax today, and that is the proudest line on the page. Everything the Commission asks for was owned at least a day ago: the panel knobs are Day 1's briefcases, the streak gate is a checkpoint reading Day 2's tally, the winning condition is one flat gate flipping a boolean the way QUIT always has, and the banner is Day 2's string racks fed to Day 3's def. Load stays level because integration replaces novelty. Day 4 is also where debugging is collected: Day 5 is presentations only, so the Commission block carries the Bug Hunt protocol and the log, and the deadline is finished, a harder word than running.

Where recruits actually stall: Block A (Drills 1 and 2, about 35 minutes) is the schematic; the stall is drawing the lock test as one box instead of four checkpoints feeding a counter, which the hinge is built to catch. Sign schematics as they come, not at the end. Block B (Drills 3 and 4 plus the hinge, about 35 minutes): the common Drill 4 error is editing a banner rack's row to a new length and leaving the old left number, which the second silent-bug warning names. Block C, the Commission, the bug hunting, and the rehearsal, needs everything that is left, at least 65 minutes; call a hard stop fifteen minutes before the bell for the rehearsal. The deadline law is real: a recruit with nothing running at the bell reverts to the clean game plus one change, and that counts. Collect the log rows as recruits finish. The classic overreach is three knob changes in one run, which the house law exists to stop.
